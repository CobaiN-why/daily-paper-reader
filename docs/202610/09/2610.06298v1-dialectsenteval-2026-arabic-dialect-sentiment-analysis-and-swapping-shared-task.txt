## Abstract 

Sentiment analysis is a fundamental problem in Natural Language Processing (NLP). Standard sentiment classification for the Arabic language remains challenging due to the high volume of dialectal Arabic. To advance research in this area, this paper proposes the Shared Task on Sentiment Analysis and Swapping in Arabic Di-alects (DialectSentEval), hosted with the Ara-bic Natural Language Processing Conference (ArabicNLP 2026). This shared task consists of two subtasks: Subtask 1 focuses on multi-class and multi-dialect sentiment analysis, re-quiring models to identify sentiment polarity across various Arabic dialects. Subtask 2 in-troduces a generative task for Arabic sentiment swap, challenging models to invert sentiment polarity while preserving core semantics. In this overview paper, we present the motivation, dataset creation, and summarize the main find-ings from participating models. 

## 1 Introduction 

Sentiment analysis remains a key task in Natu-ral Language Processing (NLP), with critical real-world applications ranging from social media moni-toring to customer feedback analysis. While signif-icant progress has been made for many languages, and multiple types have been defined (document, sentence, and feature/aspect level), the Arabic lan-guage presents unique challenges due to its rich morphology and extensive dialectal variation. Most user-generated content, the primary source for sen-timent data, is written in dialectal Arabic, which is often under-resourced and differs significantly from Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). To address these challenges and foster innova-tion in Arabic NLP, we propose a novel Shared Task on Sentiment Analysis and Swapping in Arabic Di-alects (DialectSentEval), hosted with the Arabic Natural Language Processing Conference (Arabic-NLP 2026) 1. This task is designed to move beyond standard sentiment classification by evaluating both the understanding and generation of sentiment in di-verse Arabic contexts. DialectSentEval comprises two distinct but complementary subtasks: • Subtask 1: Arabic Dialect Sentiment Anal-ysis : A multi-class classification task focused on identifying the sentiment (positive, neg-ative, or neutral) of texts written in various Arabic dialects. This subtask emphasizes the need for robust models that can handle the lex-ical and syntactic diversity of the Arab world. • Subtask 2: Arabic Sentiment Swap : A gen-erative task where participants’ systems must rewrite a given sentence to invert its senti-ment polarity (e.g., positive to negative) while preserving its core meaning. This challenges models to go beyond surface-level cues and demonstrate a deeper grasp of semantics and syntax. 

## 2 Motivation 

The motivation for DialectSentEval is grounded in both linguistic and methodological gaps in current Arabic NLP research. First, despite sustained interest in sentiment anal-ysis for Arabic, there remains a clear shortage of robust benchmarks that adequately capture dialec-tal diversity. Earlier shared tasks and evaluation campaigns (El-Beltagy et al., 2017; Rosenthal et al., 2017) have advanced sentiment classification for Arabic, yet most resources are either limited to MSA or focus on a narrow set of dialects and do-mains (El-Haj, 2026, 2020). In practice, however, the majority of user-generated content, including reviews, social media posts, and online discussions, is written in dialectal Arabic. These dialects exhibit substantial lexical, morphological, and syntactic

variation, which often leads to performance degra-dation when models trained on MSA are applied to real-world data. Prior work has highlighted the fragmentation of Arabic sentiment resources and the difficulty of transferring models across dialects (Nwesri et al., 2025; Alwakid et al., 2022), rein-forcing the need for more representative evaluation benchmarks. By introducing a balanced, multi-dialect benchmark informed by recent benchmark-ing efforts (Alharbi et al., 2025c), DialectSentEval seeks to promote the development of sentiment models that generalise across dialects and better reflect authentic language use in the Arab world. Second, the task aims to extend evaluation be-yond classification into controlled text generation. While sentiment classification has reached a rela-tively mature stage in Arabic NLP, generative sen-timent manipulation remains underexplored (Abu-dalfa et al., 2025). Sentiment swapping, framed as a form of style transfer, requires systems to in-vert polarity while preserving core propositional content. This problem has been investigated in En-glish (Shen et al., 2017; Li et al., 2018), where style transfer research has demonstrated the complexity of disentangling content from sentiment and other stylistic attributes. Comparable resources and sys-tematic evaluation frameworks are scarce for Ara-bic, limiting progress in controllable generation (Abudalfa et al., 2024; Abdu et al., 2025). The lim-ited availability of high-quality parallel datasets has constrained progress in this area. By introducing a dedicated sentiment swap subtask, DialectSentE-val encourages research on semantic preservation, polarity control, and evaluation methodologies for Arabic natural language generation. Such capabil-ities have practical implications for data augmen-tation, controllable dialogue systems, and adaptive content generation, particularly in low-resource and dialectally diverse settings. By combining a dialect-aware classification task with a challenging generative task, DialectSentEval offers a unified benchmark for both sentiment un-derstanding and sentiment manipulation in Arabic. This dual focus is intended to stimulate research that moves beyond surface-level polarity detection towards more semantically grounded and control-lable language technologies for Arabic. 

## 3 Data Collection and Creation 

The datasets for both subtasks were collected and annotated prior to the task launch. Figure 1 illus-trates the dataset creation process for both subtasks. 

3.1 Subtask 1: Dialect Sentiment Analysis 

The dataset for this task, Multi-Dialect-Sent (MDS-3), currently consists of 3,000 sentences annotated for three-class sentiment (positive, negative, neu-tral). The dataset is balanced across five major dialects: Moroccan, Egyptian, Lebanese, Jorda-nian, and Saudi. The data was sourced from hotel reviews from the ADOR dataset (Alharbi et al., 2025b), then translated and annotated by native speakers of each dialect. The sentences are parallel across dialects, with balanced sentiment representa-tion across all included dialects. An initial version of the dataset was verified in recently organised shared tasks Ahasis (Alharbi et al., 2025a) and AraSentEval (Ezzini et al., 2026). The dataset was split into 80% for training and 20% for testing. The split was performed based on sentence IDs to ensure that parallel sentences remain in the same set, to prevent potential data leakage between the training and test sets. 

3.2 Subtask 2: Sentiment Swap 

This subtask draws on a dataset built as an exten-sion of MA’AKS (Mughaus et al., 2026), compris-ing a total of 9,647 sentence-pair instances. Each pair centers on a shared topic but conveys contrast-ing sentiment orientations, with one member ex-pressing a positive stance and the other a negative one. Of the full collection, 5,331 instances (55.3%) carry a Negative label, while the remaining 4,316 (44.7%) are tagged Positive. Whereas the original MA’AKS resource was built predominantly around MSA, the present cor-pus is drawn entirely from regional Arabic dialects and spans four principal dialectal families: Gulf (5,893 pairs, 61.1% of the total), Levantine (1,655 pairs, 17.2%), Egyptian (1,561 pairs, 16.2%), and Maghrebi (538 pairs, 5.6%). To ensure reliability, every pair passed through a rigorous validation process assessing fluency, se-mantic fidelity, and the correctness of the sentiment reversal. The corpus comes with fixed training, val-idation, and test partitions, requiring models to cap-ture both the meaning and the sentiment structure of each sentence. These partitions consist of 6,752 pairs for training, 964 for validation, and 1,931 for testing. Figure 1: Data creation process for both subtasks. 

## 4 Task Description and Evaluation 

In this section, we describe in detail the subtasks that define our shared task. Participants were al-lowed to participate in either one or both sub-tasks. The official evaluation was conducted on the CodaBench platform, an open-source platform designed to organize Artificial Intelligence bench-marks by defining tasks, including datasets, submis-sion pipelines, and evaluation metrics. It provides an automated and transparent framework for evalu-ating system submissions. 

4.1 Subtask 1: Arabic Dialect Sentiment Analysis 

In this subtask, participants are required to build a system that classifies the sentiment expressed in sentences written in five different and linguistically challenging Arabic dialects: Moroccan, Egyptian, Lebanese, Jordanian, and Saudi. The goal of this task is to evaluate the ability of models to handle dialectal variation in Arabic while accurately iden-tifying the sentiment conveyed in each sentence. Formally, the objective is to design a sentence-level classifier C such that, given an input sentence 

x, the system predicts a sentiment label S, where: 

S = C(x), S ∈ { Positive , Neutral , Negative }.

• Input : A sentence written in Arabic using one of the target dialects (Moroccan, Egyptian, Lebanese, Jordanian, or Saudi). • Output : A sentiment label selected from the set {Positive, Neutral, Negative}. • Evaluation Metric : Systems are evaluated using the Macro F1-score . This metric is chosen because it gives equal importance to all classes and is therefore more suitable in scenarios where class distributions may be imbalanced. 

4.2 Subtask 2: Arabic Sentiment Swap 

In the second subtask, participants are expected to design models able to generate a new sentence that expresses the opposite sentiment of a given sen-tence while preserving its original semantic content. The input sentences are written in one of several Arabic dialect groups: Maghreb, Egyptian, Gulf, and Levantine. This task evaluates a model’s abil-ity to perform controlled sentiment transformation while maintaining semantic fidelity. Formally, the objective is to train a model M

capable of performing sentiment-controlled text generation. Given an input sentence x expressing a sentiment S, the model should generate a new sentence x′ that conveys the opposite sentiment S′,while preserving the core meaning and topic of the original sentence, such that: x′ = M (x), S′ = Opposite (S)

where S, S ′ ∈ { Positive , Negative } and S′̸ = S.• Input : An Arabic sentence along with its orig-inal sentiment polarity. • Output : A rewritten Arabic sentence that pre-serves the main meaning of the input while expressing the opposite sentiment polarity. • Evaluation Metrics : The generated outputs are evaluated using both sentiment accuracy and text similarity metrics to ensure correct sentiment transfer while maintaining semantic consistency. 

– Sentiment Style Accuracy : A pre-trained sentiment classifier from CamelLab 2 is used to measure the percentage of generated sentences that correctly express the target polarity. 

– BLEU and chrF : These metrics are used to evaluate the degree of lexical and character-level similarity between the generated sentence and the reference sentence, providing an indication of se-mantic preservation.

## 6 Results and Analysis 

6.1 Subtask 1: Arabic Dialect Sentiment Analysis 

Table 1 reports the official evaluation results on the test set for Subtask 1, ranked by Macro F1-score. 

Analysis of Subtask 1 Results. Several key find-ings emerge from the experimental outcomes of Subtask 1: 1. Pretrained Dialectal Encoders Outperform Generative LLMs : All top-performing sub-missions relied on dedicated dialectal en-coder models—most notably MARBERTv2 and AraBERTv02. Models exposed to sub-stantial dialectal social media data during pretraining consistently outperformed general Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) baselines and zero-shot prompting of large generative mod-els. 2. Robustness via Adversarial Training and Pseudo-Labeling : The winning system, 

ALEXIS , achieved a substantial lead (+4.57% Macro F1 over 2nd place). This gain was pri-marily driven by Fast Gradient Method (FGM) adversarial training, which forces the model representations to remain invariant to dialectal surface lexical variations, paired with iterative self-training that effectively bootstrapped rep-resentations for the unlabelled Lebanese test instances. 3. Ensembling and Loss Engineering : Teams that implemented stratified K-fold ensem-bling ( Axiom , faisalm93 ) and model stack-ing across distinct pretraining families ( NU-SentEval , NAMAA ) demonstrated higher sta-bility. Furthermore, incorporating Focal Loss (NU-SentEval ) effectively addressed the sub-tle semantic boundaries between neutral and mildly opinionated dialectal phrases. 4. Zero-Shot Levantine Transferability : Anal-ysis by PTUK-NLP and Lahja showed that models trained on geographically proximate dialects (Jordanian) exhibit strong zero-shot cross-lingual transfer to Lebanese, confirm-ing shared lexical roots within the Levantine dialect family.

The Polarity Transfer vs. Content Preser-vation Trade-off : Generative sentiment style transfer poses an intrinsic tension between modifying sentiment markers (necessary for high Sentiment Style Accuracy) and retain-ing non-sentiment lexical content (measured by BLEU and chrF). Filter-ing candidate rewrites with an external senti-ment classifier prevented output hallucination and ensured the target polarity was reliably achieved. 4. ## 7 Conclusion 

This overview paper presented the results and find-ings of the DialectSentEval shared task, held at ArabicNLP 2026. The shared task benchmarked two core dimensions of dialectal NLP: multi-class Arabic dialect sentiment classification (Subtask 1) and generative sentiment style transfer across four major Arabic dialect groups (Subtask 2). A total of 15 teams actively participated in the competition. The evaluation demonstrated the con-tinued superiority of dialect-pretrained masked en-coders when paired with adversarial regularization and self-training for classification, as well as the immense potential of Direct Preference Optimiza-tion (DPO) and classifier-guided reranking for con-trolled generative style transfer in low-resource and dialectally diverse environments.
